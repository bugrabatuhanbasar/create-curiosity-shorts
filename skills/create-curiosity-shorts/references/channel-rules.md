# Channel rules

## Promise
Explain an unfamiliar reason behind something recognizable in the real world. One question, an understandable answer and a visual demonstration connect three pillars.

## Pillars
- Objects: select identifiable features and demonstrate the mechanism. Verify product/model/date scope; avoid unsupported origin stories.
- Animals: use the correct species and distinguish observed behavior, anatomy, inferred function and speculation. Do not attribute intention without evidence. Real behavior footage must not be replaced with synthetic proof.
- Science and scale: state whether comparing diameter, volume, area, speed or distance. Preserve units, conditions and average/maximum definitions. Specify center-to-center vs surface gap. Calculate exact layouts rather than asking image generation for reliable ratios.

## Style
Reference early real-footage-supported explanatory Shorts, not a particular creator's wording or scene sequence. A CGI appearance does not establish AI production. Famous wealth, business scandals and miscellaneous history are adjacent curiosity topics, not automatic pillar matches.

## Editing priorities
Every shot identifies, isolates, demonstrates, compares or completes the explanation. Add zooms/arrows only to guide attention. Use real action, then a freeze/slow replay if needed. No forced cut frequency. Keep meaningful subtitle groups clear of the key detail and platform UI; inspect a phone preview rather than prescribing one fixed safe-area number. Keep narration audible; sound cues follow actual motion or emphasis. End with the promised answer; use a natural loop only if comprehension survives.

## Economics and scope
No self-filming/presenter. Use real footage/photos plus original text, narration and substantive editing. License and monetization assessment are separate; permission does not establish originality. AI is optional. Reuse assets and simple diagrams before buying clips or generating video. Templates support identity; they do not replace original explanation. Localize written labels, numbers/units and timing as well as voice. A long video needs its own coherent question and development, not simply stitched Shorts.

## Subject selection
Score 0–2 for visual clarity, narrow curiosity, explanatory payoff, source quality, asset availability and workload. Suggested editorial triage: 9–12 prioritize, 6–8 narrow, below 6 defer. Missing core evidence/visual overrides the total. These are planning heuristics, not predicted views. Candidate subjects require research before narration.
